<objective>
Cut the planet's per-pixel fragment cost from 16 to 11 worst-case value-noise evaluations (about 10.3 average) without changing the approved Lave appearance, so the full 29-test browser suite goes green in its 2-worker configuration with real headroom, and add a deterministic guard that fails if the budget ever grows again.

Purpose: G-03-3. The phase-03 gap closure raised the shader from 7 to 16 noise evaluations per pixel. Under headless SwiftShader (4 CPUs, 2 Playwright workers) that is 4.3 fps solo and 1.91-1.96 fps per page under load. The game loop deliberately caps catch-up at 5 x 1/120 s, so simulated time advances at exactly `fps x 5/120` (0.18x wall solo, 0.080-0.082x loaded). The launch/hint queue needs 12.92 s of simulated time, which is 161.5 s of wall time under load — past the test's fixed 150 s wait. Raising the wait or serialising workers would hide a 2.3x throughput regression; restoring the shader budget fixes it.

Purpose (why this arithmetic and not "24 fps"): the test does not need real-time play, it needs the drain to fit. 12.92 s / 150 s = 0.086 sim rate, i.e. 2.07 fps under load is the absolute floor. The pre-gap 7-evaluation shader ran at about 8 fps solo and the suite passed. This plan targets about 3 fps under load (drain about 107 s, test duration about 125-130 s), which is 1.4x headroom and close to the pre-gap throughput.

Output: the reduced-budget shader, the mirror constants that keep the calibration honest, a re-measured `RELIEF_SCALE`, and `tests/render/planet-budget.test.ts`.
</objective>
