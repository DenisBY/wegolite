<objective>
**As a** player on a mid-range desktop, **I want** the game to stay inside a written frame budget and to be measured on real hardware, **so that** "60 fps at 1080p" is a recorded fact rather than a hope.

Purpose: QUAL-01, decided as D-10 to D-14. The container renders on SwiftShader (a loaded in-flight frame measured 369.4 ms), so 60 fps cannot be shown there (research Pitfall 1). The proof is two parts: (a) deterministic budgets the container can gate on every run (draw calls, triangles, noise evaluations), and (b) a manual measurement on the owner's desktop at 1920x1080 and DPR 1, recorded as a frame-time distribution (D-11). The octave budget stays at 9 (D-12). The look does not change without a new owner decision (D-13). Texture compression stays deferred; this plan measures load size and records it (D-14).

Output: a per-scene budget spec, a reusable save-fixture helper, and `docs/PERFORMANCE.md` with the budget, the container numbers and the manual protocol.

Framing source: QUAL-01 and CONTEXT D-10 to D-14.

Measured this planning session (host read of the existing `dist/`, to be re-measured in the container by Task 2): the entry chunk `dist/assets/index-*.js` is 1 581 036 bytes raw and 311 353 bytes at gzip -9, above STACK.md's "Initial JS <= 250 KB gzip" target; `dist/textures` is 20 256 741 bytes and `dist/models` 368 264 bytes. The JS figure is recorded as a finding, not gated and not fixed here: it is outside QUAL-01's draw-call and triangle budget, and D-14 defers load optimisation until a stated load budget fails.
</objective>
