<design_decisions>
Fixed during planning. The executor implements these as written and cites them in code comments and in the SUMMARY.

1. Where the pre-roll lives. It is app-side: a timer in `src/app/launch-sequence.ts`, advanced once per fixed step by
   the sim driver. The drain turns a passing launch into `startPreRoll()`. While the timer runs, every fixed step is an
   ordinary docked sim step. On the fixed step that ends it, the driver calls `autosave()` and then steps the sim with
   the launch command first in that step's commands. The sim gets exactly one launch command, at the moment the
   pre-roll ends. Nothing in `src/sim/**` changes.
2. Manual clock. The length is a getter: `LAUNCH_PRE_ROLL_DURATION` in a real-time session and 0 under
   `?test=1&clock=manual`. With length 0, `startPreRoll()` returns false and the drain keeps today's path (autosave in
   the drain, launch forwarded to the first step). Reason: D-09 says `advanceTicks(n)` steps exactly n sim ticks, and
   the hash-pinned twins launch on tick 1. Under the manual clock the launch view starts at view time
   `LAUNCH_PRE_ROLL_DURATION`, the same pose a real-time pre-roll ends on. This is recorded as part of the PROJECT.md
   amendment and is reversible.
3. One view timeline. View time tau runs from 0 at the pre-roll start to
   `LAUNCH_VIEW_DURATION = LAUNCH_PRE_ROLL_DURATION + LAUNCH_DURATION` at handover. While pre-rolling,
   tau = `preRollViewTime(preRollElapsed, alpha)`. That is the elapsed time minus `(1 - alpha) * DT`, clamped to
   [0, LAUNCH_PRE_ROLL_DURATION], with the same non-finite handling as `launchViewElapsed`. While launching,
   tau = `LAUNCH_PRE_ROLL_DURATION + launchViewElapsed(launchElapsed, alpha)`. The step that ends the pre-roll is the
   launch step, which does not move the ship (`step.ts` block 3), so the drawn tau holds at the boundary for one
   1/120 s step. That hold already existed at the start of the l26 launch view. It is accepted and is not a test target.
4. The axial curve. `launchViewAxial(slotDepth, launchSpeed, tau)` gives the camera's signed distance from the dock
   mouth along `layout.stationToPlanet`, negative inside the slot. Definitions:
   - `lead = -VIEW_OFFSETS.fore.z`, `start = -slotDepth`, `end = launchSpeed * LAUNCH_DURATION + lead`,
     `span = end - start`, `m = launchSpeed * LAUNCH_VIEW_DURATION / span`.
   - `u = clamp(tau / LAUNCH_VIEW_DURATION, 0, 1)`, `g(u) = (4 - m) * u^3 + (m - 3) * u^4`,
     `axial = start + span * g(u)`.

   Properties: it starts at the full depth at rest with zero acceleration, `g(1) = 1`, and `g'(1) = m`, so it ends at
   the cockpit's handover position with the ship's speed. It is monotone for m < 4, and the camera never passes the
   cockpit for m < 2; the shipped constant gives m of about 0.97. This quartic replaces l26's smoothstep. The smoothest
   alternative, a cubic Hermite, leaves the slot after about 1.8 s. Any curve that keeps the walls on screen longer must
   exit faster (see 7).
5. The camera. `computeSlotLaunchCamera(layout, slotDepth, launchSpeed, position, orientation, tau)` returns the fore
   cockpit pose of (position, orientation), moved back along `layout.stationToPlanet` by
   `pullback = lead + launchSpeed * max(0, tau - LAUNCH_PRE_ROLL_DURATION) - launchViewAxial(...)`. The middle term is
   the nominal cockpit position: parked during the pre-roll, at the ship's speed afterwards. So the camera's axial
   position is the curve, the pullback is exactly 0 at handover, and the velocity is continuous at the boundary even
   though the hull goes from parked to the launch speed there. If the depth is undefined, not finite or not positive,
   or the speed is not finite or is negative, the function returns the plain cockpit pose (as l26 does now).
6. The pre-roll pose. `preRollPose(layout, dock, tau)` returns position `dockMouthPosition(layout)` and orientation
   `normalize(mul(launchOrientation(layout, dock), axisAngle(0, 0, 1, launchViewStationAngle(layout, tau))))`, with
   `launchViewStationAngle(layout, tau) = layout.stationRollRadPerSec * (tau - LAUNCH_PRE_ROLL_DURATION)`. The angle is
   negative during the pre-roll and 0 at the launch step. While launching it equals the sim's `stationAngle` at the
   interpolated time, as l26 pinned. At tau = LAUNCH_PRE_ROLL_DURATION the pose is exactly what `beginLaunch` sets, and
   the camera rolls with the station, so the walls hold still (D-10).
7. Expected values with the shipped data (depth 250 m, launch speed 175 m/s, lead 31 m). Use them to check the
   implementation and in the SUMMARY; never write them into code.
   - Curve: span 631 m, m 0.97068.
   - Axial position: -250.0 m at tau 0, -233.9 m at 0.75 s, -142.7 m at 1.5 s (speed 185.7 m/s), +381.0 m at 3.5 s
     (speed 175.0 m/s).
   - The camera crosses the mouth at tau 2.111 s. Peak speed is 304.3 m/s at tau 2.61 s.
   - On a 1280x720 view (half-angle tangents 0.545 wide and 0.307 high; slot half-extents 120.9 and 51.7 m; camera
     7.25 m toward +i), the top wall leaves the frame when the camera is about 145 m from the mouth, which is tau of
     about 1.49 s on this curve. The camera stays inside the slot for about 2.1 s, but the walls are on screen for about
     1.5 s.
   - The SUMMARY reports what the frames show. If the walls are clearly gone before 2 s, it records an owner question:
     raise `LAUNCH_PRE_ROLL_DURATION`, or accept a faster exit after the walls. Do not change the curve for it.
8. Presentation during the pre-roll. When the pre-roll starts (the drain, through a new sim-driver dep
   `onPreRollStart`), the session hides the station shell, switches the input context to `flight` and syncs the
   station nav, which is the UI half of what the "launched" handler does now. While it runs:
   - the render driver draws (the redraw gate does not treat a pre-roll as docked);
   - the camera is the pre-roll pose path and the station is rolled at the view time;
   - `#hud` is hidden and has `data-launching`, so the D-02 rule keeps the overlay's version and system lines hidden;
   - the Cobra stays hidden;
   - the nav state reports `launching`;
   - the probe publishes `launch.preRolling` and `launch.preRollElapsed`.

   The probe's `mode` stays the sim mode (`docked`).
9. Skip and pause. Pause needs nothing new: the pre-roll advances only inside fixed steps, and a paused frame takes
   none. The skip key, after the first viewing, calls `skipPreRoll()`. The next fixed step ends the pre-roll; the
   driver autosaves and takes the launch step, then pushes `{ kind: "skipLaunch" }` onto `pending`, so the next frame
   skips the sim launch exactly as the existing key path does (D-10, +600 s once). `skipPreRoll()` is ignored before
   `markSeen()`, so the first viewing always plays in full.
10. Safety. If the pre-roll is running but the state is no longer docked on the home screen (a restore, a new game),
    the per-step wrapper cancels it and steps normally. `reset()` (called by the "launched" and "docked" handlers) also
    ends any pre-roll. `startPreRoll()` resets `elapsed` and `complete`, so `canSkip` reads a fresh launch, and it
    never restarts a pre-roll that is already running.
</design_decisions>
